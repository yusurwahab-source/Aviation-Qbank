Know the system, not the question.

Every fact below is a statement of how the system works. Read a section, then sit its questions: each wrong option can be ruled out by understanding the system, not by recognising its shape.

For study onlyThis guide and its questions are a study aid. They must not be used operationally without checking the FCOM of the aircraft you fly. Figures, logic and procedures differ between operators, aircraft standards and modifications (the A320ceo and A320neo differ in places). Your operator's FCOM always takes precedence.

21 Air Conditioning 33 questions

Two packs turn hot bleed air into cool, dry air; a mixer unit blends it with recirculated cabin air; trim air warms individual zones. Learn the chain in order — source, pack, mixer, zone — and every fault becomes a question of where in the chain the air stops.

Zones

  • Which three temperature zones have their own selector on the A320 AIR COND panel? Cockpit, forward cabin and aft cabin. The panel carries COCKPIT, FWD CABIN and AFT CABIN selectors. Cargo has its own ventilation and is not one of the three conditioned zones.
  • What temperature does a zone selector request in its 12 o'clock position? 24 °C (76 °F). The mid position is about 24 °C. The scale runs from about 18 °C at COLD to about 30 °C at HOT.
  • What temperature does a zone selector request at the COLD stop? 18 °C (64 °F). COLD is about 18 °C. The selectors cover roughly 18–30 °C, with 24 °C at the top.
  • What temperature does a zone selector request at the HOT stop? 30 °C (86 °F). HOT is about 30 °C. The selectors cover roughly 18–30 °C, with 24 °C at the top.

Packs

  • How do PACK 1 and PACK 2 operate in normal use? Automatically and independently of each other. Each pack has its own controller and flow control valve; the loss of one does not take the other with it.
  • What does the PACK pushbutton in the ON (lights out) position do? The pack flow control valve is automatically controlled. ON hands the valve to automatic control. The valve still closes automatically for its protections.
  • What happens when a PACK pushbutton is selected OFF? The pack flow control valve closes. Selecting OFF shuts the pack's flow control valve, so that pack delivers no air.
  • Which condition does NOT turn on the amber PACK FAULT light? The PACK FLOW selector set to LO. FAULT means a valve disagreement or a pack overheat (compressor outlet or pack outlet). Flow selection is a normal setting, not a fault.

Pack flow

  • What flow does the PACK FLOW selector give in LO? 80 % of normal. LO is 80 %, NORM 100 % and HI 120 %.
  • What flow does the PACK FLOW selector give in HI? 120 % of normal. HI is 120 %, used for smoke removal and hot or wet conditions.
  • In which situation is the PACK FLOW selector setting irrelevant, the flow being HI regardless? Single pack operation, or when the APU bleed is supplying the packs. With only one pack, or with APU bleed air, the system always uses HI flow; the selector has no effect.
  • LO pack flow is selected, but the cooling demand cannot be met. What does the system do? It goes to 100 % flow even though LO is selected. LO is a request, not a limit: if cooling cannot be satisfied the flow rises to NORM.
  • On what basis is the PACK FLOW selector set? The number of passengers and the ambient conditions (smoke removal, hot or wet conditions). Fewer passengers allow LO to save bleed air; smoke removal and hot or humid days call for HI.

Trim air

  • What does the HOT AIR pushbutton in the ON position provide? Hot air for the trim air system. HOT AIR ON opens the hot air pressure regulating valve so trim air is available to raise each zone's temperature.
  • What happens when the HOT AIR pushbutton is selected OFF? The hot air valve closes and any FAULT is reset. OFF closes the hot air valve; it is also the action that clears a HOT AIR FAULT.
  • At what duct temperature does the HOT AIR FAULT light come on? Above 88 °C (190 °F). A duct overheat above 88 °C gives the amber FAULT and an ECAM caution, and the valves close automatically.
  • After a HOT AIR FAULT, when does the FAULT light go out by itself? When the duct temperature falls below 70 °C (158 °F). The light goes out below 70 °C, or when the crew selects the pushbutton OFF.

Emergency ram air

  • What does pressing the guarded RAM AIR pushbutton ON do? It opens the emergency ram air inlet. RAM AIR is a separate emergency inlet, used to ventilate the aircraft when the packs are lost or for smoke removal.
  • RAM AIR is selected ON while the cabin differential pressure is 1 psi or more. What happens? The outflow valve control stays normal and no emergency ram air flows in. Ram air pressure cannot overcome a cabin at 1 psi or more above ambient, so nothing flows until the differential falls.
  • RAM AIR is ON, differential pressure is below 1 psi and pressurisation is in automatic. What does the outflow valve do? It opens to about 50 %. Opening the outflow valve about halfway gives the incoming ram air a way out, so it flows through the cabin.
  • RAM AIR is ON with pressurisation in manual mode. What does the outflow valve do automatically? Nothing: it does not open automatically under manual control. In MAN the crew drives the outflow valve with MAN V/S CTL; the automatic opening only happens under automatic control.
  • Where does emergency ram air go once it flows in? Directly to the mixer unit. Emergency ram air bypasses the packs and enters the mixer unit, from where it is distributed to the zones.
  • What does DITCHING do to the emergency ram air inlet? It closes it, even if RAM AIR is ON. Ditching closes every opening below the floating line, the ram air inlet included.

Indications

  • What does a green ALTN MODE message on the ECAM COND page mean? The primary zone controller is faulty. ALTN MODE shows that the zone controller's primary channel has failed and its back-up is in use.
  • What does a green PACK REG message on the ECAM COND page mean? The zone controller is faulty, so the packs regulate the temperature themselves. With the zone controller lost, the pack controllers take over temperature regulation on their own.
  • What does an amber FAN on the ECAM COND page indicate? A recirculation fan is faulty. The FAN indication on the COND page concerns the cabin recirculation fans.
  • Above what duct temperature does a zone's duct temperature turn amber on the COND page? 80 °C. The duct indication turns amber above 80 °C — an early sign of trim air heating too much.
  • On the COND page, the hot air pressure regulating valve shows an amber crossline. What does that mean? HOT AIR is OFF with the valve closed, or the valve disagrees with its commanded position. A green crossline is a normally closed valve with the button in AUTO; amber flags OFF or a disagreement.
  • Above what temperature does the pack outlet temperature turn amber on the BLEED page? 90 °C. Pack outlet above 90 °C is an overheat indication.
  • Above what temperature does the pack compressor outlet temperature turn amber on the BLEED page? 230 °C. The compressor outlet is the hottest point inside the pack; amber above 230 °C indicates an overheat.
  • On the BLEED page, how is a pack ram air inlet flap that is fully open on the ground shown? Amber inline. Fully open in flight is green; fully open on the ground is amber.
  • What do the letters C and H next to a pack's turbine bypass valve on the BLEED page mean? C: the valve is closed (cold); H: the valve is open (hot). The bypass valve sends hot air around the cooling turbine. Closed gives the coldest outlet, open the hottest.
  • When is the pack flow indication shown in amber on the BLEED page? When the pack flow control valve is closed. Amber pack flow means no flow through that pack because its flow control valve is closed.

21 Pressurization 31 questions

The packs push air in, one outflow valve lets it out, and two pressure controllers decide how far the valve is open, following a schedule from the ground through climb, cruise and descent. Two safety valves stand behind it.

Modes

  • In which ways can the flight crew run the cabin pressurisation system? Automatically, semi-automatically (manual landing elevation) or manually. Normal operation is fully automatic. Setting LDG ELEV by hand is the semi-automatic case; MODE SEL MAN is full manual control.

Schedule

  • On the ground after landing, how long after touchdown does the outflow valve go fully open? 55 seconds. On the ground before take-off and 55 s after landing the outflow valve opens fully so no residual pressure remains.
  • At touchdown, at what cabin vertical speed is any remaining cabin pressure released? 500 ft/min. The residual differential is let out at a controlled 500 ft/min so the ears of the passengers are not hit by a sudden change.
  • During take-off, at what rate does the system pre-pressurise the cabin? 400 ft/min. Pre-pressurisation runs at 400 ft/min (cabin descending) to avoid a pressure surge at rotation.
  • Pre-pressurisation continues until the differential pressure reaches what value? 0.1 psi. A small positive differential of 0.1 psi is enough to stop the pressure bump when the aircraft rotates.
  • Why does the system pre-pressurise the cabin for take-off? To avoid a pressure surge at rotation. Without a small positive differential the cabin would see a pressure bump as the aircraft rotates.
  • When does the pressure controller begin its climb phase? At lift-off. The climb phase starts at lift-off, following on from pre-pressurisation.
  • In the climb, how does the cabin altitude change? According to a pre-programmed law that takes the aircraft's actual rate of climb into account. The cabin climbs on a schedule that adapts to how fast the aircraft is actually climbing.
  • What cabin altitude does the system hold in cruise? The level-off value or the landing field elevation, whichever is higher. If the destination is higher than the cruise cabin altitude, the system holds the destination elevation instead.
  • What does the controller aim for during the descent? Cabin pressure equal to the landing field pressure just before landing. Matching field pressure at touchdown means nothing is left to release when the doors open.
  • What is the maximum cabin rate of descent in automatic control? 750 ft/min. The descent rate is limited to 750 ft/min for passenger comfort.
  • During a rejected take-off, what does the pressurisation system do? It stops the cabin altitude climbing and sets cabin pressure back to take-off altitude + 0.1 psi. The abort phase holds the cabin at the pre-pressurised take-off condition instead of starting a climb.

Controls

  • With LDG ELEV in AUTO, where does the landing elevation come from? From the FMGS, which is used to build an optimised pressure schedule. In AUTO the destination data in the FMGS drive the schedule.
  • What range of landing elevation can the crew select with the LDG ELEV selector? –2 000 ft to +14 000 ft. Any position other than AUTO uses the selected value as the landing elevation reference.
  • How should the crew set an accurate manual landing elevation with the LDG ELEV selector? By reading the value on the ECAM, because the selector scale is only indicative. The knob's scale is approximate; the ECAM PRESS page shows the value actually selected.
  • How does the MODE SEL pushbutton show that manual mode is selected? MAN appears in white, and FAULT does not come on. MAN is a crew choice, so it is white. Amber FAULT is reserved for the failure of both automatic systems.
  • When does the MODE SEL FAULT light come on? When both automatic pressure control systems are faulty. One failed system is covered by the other. FAULT with an ECAM caution means both are lost and manual control is needed.
  • With MODE SEL in MAN, what does holding the MAN V/S CTL switch UP do? Moves the outflow valve towards open. UP opens the valve, so the cabin altitude rises. DN closes it.
  • With MODE SEL in MAN, what does holding the MAN V/S CTL switch DN do? Moves the outflow valve towards closed. DN closes the valve, so cabin pressure rises and cabin altitude comes down.
  • Why must the MAN V/S CTL switch be held rather than flicked? The outflow valve moves slowly, so the switch is held until the target vertical speed is reached. The valve travel is slow; short inputs barely move it.
  • Which of these is NOT closed by the DITCHING pushbutton? The engine bleed valves. Ditching closes the openings to the outside: outflow valve, emergency ram air inlet, avionics ventilation inlet and extract valves, and pack flow control valves.

Indications

  • On the PRESS page, when are the LDG ELEV mode and value hidden? When MODE SEL is in MAN. In manual mode the controller is not using a landing elevation, so it is not displayed.
  • Above what cabin vertical speed does the V/S turn amber on the PRESS page? 2 000 ft/min. Amber above 2 000 ft/min. It begins pulsing green above 1 800 ft/min.
  • Above what cabin vertical speed does the V/S indication start pulsing? 1 800 ft/min (it resets at 1 600 ft/min). Pulsing is an advisory before the 2 000 ft/min amber limit.
  • Above what differential pressure does ΔP turn amber on the PRESS page? 8.5 psi. ΔP turns amber above 8.5 psi (and also when it goes slightly negative).
  • Above what cabin altitude is the cabin altitude shown in red on the PRESS page? 9 550 ft. Red above 9 550 ft corresponds to the cabin altitude warning.
  • From what cabin altitude does the cabin altitude indication pulse? 8 800 ft. Pulsing from 8 800 ft gives warning before the 9 550 ft red limit.
  • On the PRESS page, what does SYS 1 shown in amber mean? System 1 is the active system and it is faulty. The active system is shown in green normally and in amber when it is faulty.
  • What does a green MAN on the PRESS page system indication show? MODE SEL is in MAN. With manual mode selected the system indication reads MAN.
  • When is SAFETY shown in amber on the PRESS page? When a safety valve is not fully closed. Normally the safety valves are closed and SAFETY is white. An open safety valve is abnormal.
  • When is the outflow valve diagram shown in amber in flight? When the valve is open more than 95 %. In flight a nearly fully open outflow valve means the cabin cannot hold pressure.

21 Ventilation 16 questions

Avionics ventilation picks its own configuration — open circuit on the ground, closed circuit in flight — and the crew can override it for smoke. Cargo is ventilated by an extraction fan drawing cabin air through it.

Avionics ventilation

  • With BLOWER and EXTRACT in AUTO, what avionics ventilation configuration is used on the ground before take-off power is set (normal skin temperature)? Open circuit. On the ground before take-off power the system runs open circuit, drawing air in and sending it overboard.
  • On the ground before take-off power, when does the avionics ventilation use the closed circuit instead of the open circuit? When the skin temperature is below the ground threshold. A cold skin can cool the recirculated air on its own, so there is no need to take outside air.
  • With both switches in AUTO, what configuration is used after take-off power is applied and in flight? Closed circuit. In flight the cold skin cools the recirculated avionics air, so the closed circuit is used.
  • What happens when either the BLOWER or the EXTRACT pushbutton is set to OVRD? The system goes to closed circuit and air from the air conditioning system is added to the ventilation air. OVRD on one switch brings conditioned air into the closed avionics circuit.
  • What happens to the blower fan when the BLOWER pushbutton is set to OVRD? It stops. With BLOWER in OVRD the blower fan stops and conditioned air takes its place.
  • With both BLOWER and EXTRACT set to OVRD, how does the ventilation air flow? From the air conditioning system, through the avionics, and then overboard, with the extract fan still running. This is the configuration used for avionics smoke: conditioned air in, straight out overboard.

Faults

  • Which of the following does NOT turn on the BLOWER FAULT light? A pack overheat. BLOWER FAULT comes from low blowing pressure, duct overheat, computer power failure or the smoke warning.
  • Which of the following turns on the EXTRACT FAULT light? Low extract pressure. EXTRACT FAULT comes from low extract pressure, computer power failure or the smoke warning.
  • When the avionics smoke warning is active, what do the BLOWER and EXTRACT pushbuttons show? FAULT on both. The smoke warning lights both FAULT lights, prompting the crew to set them to OVRD in the procedure.

Cabin

  • How many cabin (recirculation) fans does the CAB FANS pushbutton control? Two. CAB FANS ON runs both cabin fans; OFF stops both.

Cargo

  • How are the cargo compartments ventilated? An extraction fan draws air out of the compartment and overboard, and cabin air flows in to replace it. The extract fan creates the flow; the supply is cabin air.
  • With the AFT ISOL VALVE pushbutton in AUTO, when does the cargo extraction fan run? When no smoke is detected in the aft cargo compartment. In AUTO the inlet and outlet isolation valves open and the fan runs unless smoke is detected.
  • What does setting the AFT ISOL VALVE pushbutton OFF close? The inlet and outlet isolation valves and the cargo trim air valve, and the extraction fan stops. OFF isolates the compartment completely, including the hot trim air used for cargo heating.
  • What turns on the AFT ISOL VALVE FAULT light? An inlet or outlet isolation valve not in the selected position. FAULT is a valve disagreement, with an ECAM caution.

Indications

  • When is VENT shown in amber on the PRESS page? When there is a BLOWER FAULT, an EXTRACT FAULT or an AVNCS SYS FAULT. Avionics ventilation is displayed on the PRESS page, below the cabin pressure data.
  • On the PRESS page, what does an amber XX on the avionics inlet or extract valve mean? The valve position is unavailable or the data are inconsistent. XX is always 'no valid data' on the ECAM.

22 Auto Flight - FCU and FMA 59 questions

Push for managed, pull for selected. The FMA is the truth about what the aircraft is doing: column by column, colour by colour. The autoland warning light belongs here too.

FCU

  • What is the FCU's role? The short-term interface between the crew and the FMGC for engaging AP/FD/A/THR, selecting modes and setting targets. Long-term inputs (flight plan, performance) go through the MCDU.
  • How does an FCU window show that its parameter is managed? Three dashes and a dot. In selected mode it shows the selected value and no dot.
  • What does the FCU altitude window always show? An altitude selected by the pilot, never dashes (the dot means managed altitude). Unlike the other windows, ALT never dashes.
  • What does pushing an FCU knob (SPD, HDG, ALT or V/S) do? Gives the corresponding mode to the FMGS to manage. Push = managed, pull = selected, turn = change the value.
  • What does pulling an FCU knob do? Uses the crew-selected value for that mode. Pull = selected.
  • In managed lateral mode, how long does a heading pre-selected by turning the HDG knob remain displayed before the dashes return? 45 seconds. The same 45 s applies to the V/S window; the SPD/MACH window shows a pre-selection for 10 s.
  • In managed speed, how long does a speed pre-selected on the SPD/MACH knob remain displayed before the dashes return? 10 seconds. The HDG/TRK and V/S windows show pre-selections for 45 s.
  • What is the range of the FCU speed window? 100 to 399 kt (Mach 0.10 to 0.99). The SPD/MACH pushbutton toggles between speed and Mach targets.
  • What is the range of the FCU altitude window? 100 to 49 000 ft. The outer knob changes the step between 100 ft and 1 000 ft.
  • What is the range of the FCU vertical speed window? –6 000 to +6 000 ft/min. In FPA mode the range is –9.9° to +9.9°.
  • What is the range of the FCU flight path angle (FPA) target? –9.9° to +9.9°. FPA replaces V/S when TRK-FPA is selected.
  • What does pushing the V/S-FPA knob do? Commands an immediate level off (V/S = 0). The FMA then shows V/S = 0.
  • What does the HDG V/S - TRK FPA pushbutton change on the PFD? It shows or removes the flight path vector and switches the FD crossbars to the flight path director. It also changes the FCU windows between heading/track and V/S/FPA.
  • What does the METRIC ALT pushbutton do? Shows the altitude target in metres on the ECAM, and altitudes in metres on the PFD. It helps in airspace that uses metric levels.

Autopilot

  • What does a green light on an AP1 or AP2 pushbutton show? That autopilot is engaged. The lights are green when engaged.
  • In normal procedures, how is the autopilot disengaged? With the takeover pushbutton on the sidestick. The sidestick pushbutton gives only short warnings.
  • How long does the cavalry charge last when the autopilot is disengaged with the sidestick takeover pushbutton? About 1.5 seconds. The master warning lasts about 3 s and the red AP OFF message about 9 s.
  • Which way of disengaging the autopilot gives continuous warnings with AUTO FLT AP OFF? Pressing the AP pushbutton on the FCU, a failure, or a force on the sidestick. Only the takeover pushbutton gives the short, self-cancelling warnings.

Autothrust

  • What does a green light on the FCU A/THR pushbutton show? Autothrust is armed or active. The FMA tells which: A/THR in blue is armed, in white active.
  • In normal procedures, how is the autothrust disconnected? With the instinctive disconnect pushbutton on the thrust levers, or by setting both levers to idle. The FCU button is not the normal way of disconnecting.

Vertical modes

  • What does the EXPED pushbutton do? Climbs or descends to the selected altitude with the maximum vertical gradient. It disregards speed and altitude constraints and the speed limit.
  • What target speed does EXP CLB use? Green dot. Green dot is held with pitch; A/THR sets climb thrust.
  • What target speed does EXP DES use? 340 kt or Mach 0.8. The speed is held with pitch while A/THR sets idle.
  • When is SRS displayed in the vertical column? When take-off or go-around mode is engaged. SRS guides pitch to hold a speed defined by the SRS law.
  • What is the difference between CLB and OP CLB? CLB respects altitude constraints; OP CLB disregards them. OP CLB climbs straight to the FCU altitude.
  • What does ALT* on the FMA mean? Altitude capture of the FCU-selected altitude. ALT CST* is the capture of an altitude constraint.
  • What does ALT CRZ mean? Altitude hold at the cruise flight level. It is the altitude hold mode engaged at the cruise FL.
  • When does the V/S annunciation flash in an amber box? When speed reaches VLS or VMAX and the vertical speed can no longer be held. V/S has priority over speed, so the speed limit eventually stops it.
  • What does a magenta ALT in the armed vertical modes mean? The target altitude is an altitude constraint. Blue ALT means the FCU altitude is the target.

Approach

  • If an ILS approach is in the flight plan, what modes does pressing APPR arm? LOC and G/S. For a non-precision approach it arms APP NAV and FINAL.
  • What does the LOC pushbutton do? Arms, engages or disengages localizer mode only. LOC alone is used for a localizer-only approach or to intercept before the glideslope.
  • What does the approach capability (CAT 1/2/3) on the FMA describe? The aircraft's capability, independent of the airport and runway. It reflects available equipment and redundancy.
  • What does CAT 3 SINGLE mean? CAT 3 capability with fail-passive condition (one autopilot). DUAL is fail-operational, with both autopilots.
  • What does CAT 3 DUAL mean? CAT 3 capability with fail-operational condition (both autopilots). A single failure still allows the autoland to be completed.
  • Where does RADIO followed by a value on the FMA come from? The minimum descent height entered in the RADIO field of the MCDU APPR PERF page. BARO shows a minimum descent altitude instead.

FMA

  • How many columns does the FMA have? Five. Autothrust, vertical, lateral, approach capability and engagement status.
  • On the FMA, what colour is an active mode? Green. Armed modes are cyan; a changed mode has a white box for 10 seconds.
  • How long is a newly changed mode surrounded by a white box on the FMA? 10 seconds. The box draws the eye to the change.

Autothrust modes

  • When is MAN TOGA shown on the FMA? When autothrust is armed and at least one thrust lever is in the TO/GA detent. It is white in a white box: thrust is set manually by lever position.
  • In MAN FLX XX, what does XX show? The flex take-off temperature, in blue. MAN FLX shows when a lever is at FLX/MCT with A/THR armed.
  • When does MAN THR appear in an amber box? When A/THR is armed and the most forward lever is above the climb detent but not in a detent. Thrust is set by a lever between detents.
  • When is THR LVR shown? When autothrust is in thrust mode with both levers below the climb detent. Thrust is limited to the lever position.
  • What does a flashing LVR CLB request? That the pilots set the thrust levers to the climb detent. It appears at the thrust reduction altitude after take-off.
  • When does LVR ASYM appear in amber? When one thrust lever is in the climb or FLX/MCT detent and the other is not (two engines). It warns of thrust levers set in different detents.
  • What does a flashing amber THR LK mean? Autothrust has been disconnected and thrust is frozen at its last value. Moving the thrust levers releases the lock.
  • If the crew takes no action with THR LK displayed, what happens? The master caution flashes and a single chime sounds every 5 seconds. The same reminder applies to LVR CLB and LVR MCT.
  • How is an armed autobrake mode shown in the FMA first column? BRK LO or BRK MED in cyan (BRK MAX on the third line). An active autobrake mode is green in a white box.

Lateral modes

  • What lateral guidance does RWY mode provide? Guidance on the take-off roll up to 30 ft RA, if a localizer signal is available. Above 30 ft it becomes RWY TRK.
  • When is RWY TRK shown? When runway mode continues once airborne, above 30 ft RA. The aircraft holds the track it had at lift-off.
  • What does NAV shown in blue mean? NAV mode is armed. It turns green when engaged.
  • When is APP NAV shown? When NAV mode is armed or engaged during a non-ILS approach. It is the lateral part of a managed non-precision approach.
  • Which lateral mode engages on a go-around? GA TRK. It holds the track at go-around initiation.

Engagement

  • On the FMA, what does 1FD1 mean? Flight director 2 has failed but both FDs are selected on. Both PFDs then show FD 1's orders.
  • What does A/THR shown in blue in the FMA fifth column mean? Autothrust is armed but not active, so thrust follows lever position. White A/THR means active.
  • Which condition, together with at least one FD on and the levers in FLX, arms autothrust? A FLEX temperature entered in the MCDU. Without a FLEX temperature, the FLX detent will not arm A/THR.
  • Pushing the A/THR pushbutton engages autothrust when the levers are where? In the engagement range: below the climb detent (or below MCT with one engine out). Alpha floor also engages autothrust.

Autoland warning

  • Below what height does the red AUTOLAND warning light operate in LAND mode? 200 ft RA. It flashes with a triple click.
  • Which localizer deviation triggers the AUTOLAND warning (above 15 ft RA)? 1/4 dot. A glide deviation of 1 dot above 100 ft RA also triggers it.
  • Which radio altimeter disagreement triggers the AUTOLAND warning? More than 15 ft between the radio altimeters. Losing both APs below 200 ft also triggers it.

22 Auto Flight - Flight Management 32 questions

The INIT, PROG, FUEL PRED and RAD NAV pages: what each field means and how the FMGC computes its fuel figures.

INIT A

  • What two main things is the MCDU INIT A page used for? Initialising the flight plan and aligning the inertial reference system. Weights and fuel go on INIT B.
  • When does the ALTN/CO RTE field on INIT A become available? Once a primary destination is entered in FROM/TO. A preferred alternate stored with the destination is shown automatically.
  • If no cruise flight level is entered on INIT A, what is the consequence on the ground? The system provides no predictions. The cruise temperature, if not entered, is assumed to be ISA.
  • What happens when a new FROM/TO pair is entered on INIT A? Any previously entered route is deleted and the route selection page is called. A new city pair starts a new flight plan.
  • After engine start, where does an uplinked flight plan from INIT REQUEST go? To the secondary flight plan, to be inserted or rejected manually. Before engine start it is inserted automatically if no active flight plan exists.
  • What is the default tropopause altitude on INIT A? 36 090 ft. It can be modified up to 60 000 ft.

INIT B

  • When does the INIT B page automatically change to the FUEL PRED page? After the first engine is started. From then on the FMGC uses fuel on board for its predictions.
  • What is the usual default taxi fuel value on INIT B? 200 kg (400 lb). It can be adjusted.
  • Before any entry, what does FINAL TIME typically default to on INIT B? 30 minutes. The default comes from the airline fuel policy.
  • For the final reserve computation, what holding conditions does the FMGS assume by default? 1 500 ft AGL, CONF 1, at maximum endurance speed. These defaults can be changed in the airline fuel policy.
  • By default, what is the MIN DEST FOB equal to? ALTN fuel + FINAL fuel. Entering a lower value triggers CHECK MIN DEST FOB.
  • What must be entered on INIT B to obtain a speed profile and predictions? ZFW and ZFWCG. Block fuel is also a mandatory entry.
  • When the block fuel is entered on INIT B, what does the page title change to? INIT FUEL PREDICTION. Using FUEL PLANNING instead changes it to INIT FUEL PLANNING.
  • On INIT B, how is a headwind entered in the TRIP WIND field? With a prefix –, H or HD. +, T or TL means a tailwind.
  • How is EXTRA fuel computed on INIT B? BLOCK – (TAXI + TRIP + RSV + MIN DEST FOB). After engine start the same formula uses FOB instead of BLOCK.

PROG

  • On the PROG page, what happens to the CRZ FL if the crew sets a higher altitude on the FCU? It is adjusted up to the new altitude and a scratchpad message appears. CRZ cannot be lower than the FCU altitude.
  • What minimum estimated cruise time does the FMGC assume when computing OPT FL? 15 minutes. OPT depends on weight, cost index, temperature and wind.

24 Electrical 52 questions

Two engine generators, the APU generator and external power, in a fixed order of priority; bus ties let one source feed the whole network. The emergency generator, the batteries and the static inverter keep the essential buses alive.

General

  • With every switch on the ELEC panel in AUTO (no lights), what is the only manual input normally needed? Pressing EXT PWR to connect ground power. The electrical system is automatic for normal operation; connecting ground power is the one routine action.

Priorities

  • Which sources do GEN 1 and GEN 2 have priority over when they are operating? Both the APU generator and external power. Engine generators come first, then external power, then the APU generator.
  • With the EXT PWR pushbutton ON, which source has priority between external power and the APU generator? External power. External power has priority over the APU generator; the engine generators have priority over both.
  • Can a single engine generator supply the entire electrical network? Yes, one engine generator can supply the whole network. Each generator is sized for the whole aircraft; the bus ties connect it to the other side. Galley loads are shed in this case.

Batteries

  • What is the normal voltage range shown on the BAT 1 and BAT 2 indicators? 25 to 31 V. The battery voltage indicators sit above the BAT pushbuttons; normal is 25–31 V.
  • What do the BAT 1 and BAT 2 pushbuttons control? Their battery charge limiters. In AUTO each charge limiter connects or disconnects its battery from the DC BAT BUS automatically.
  • In AUTO, the batteries are connected to the DC BAT BUS during which phase involving the APU? While the APU is starting (MASTER SW ON and APU speed below 95 %). The batteries help power the APU start and are disconnected again once it is up to speed.
  • Below what battery voltage does the charge limiter connect a battery to charge it? 26.5 V. A battery below 26.5 V is connected to the DC BAT BUS so it can be charged.
  • With AC BUS 1 and 2 lost, below what speed are the batteries connected to the DC BAT BUS because the emergency generator is not supplying? 100 kt. Below 100 kt the RAT cannot drive the emergency generator, so the batteries take over.
  • With no AC BUS 1 or 2 and no emergency generator, what does battery 1 supply? The AC STAT INV BUS, and the AC ESS BUS through the static inverter above 50 kt. Battery 1 drives the static inverter; battery 2 supplies the DC ESS BUS.
  • With no AC BUS 1 or 2 and no emergency generator, what does battery 2 supply? The DC ESS BUS. Battery 2 feeds DC essential; battery 1 feeds the static inverter side.
  • What happens when a BAT pushbutton is selected OFF? The battery line contactor opens; the hot buses stay supplied. OFF opens the line contactor (white OFF light when the DC BAT BUS is supplied), but the hot buses remain connected to the battery.
  • What causes a BAT FAULT light? The battery charging current is outside limits, and the battery contactor opens. An abnormal charging current is a sign of a battery problem, so the battery is isolated.

External power

  • When does the green EXT PWR AVAIL light come on? When external power is plugged in and its parameters are normal. AVAIL means ground power is ready; pressing the button then connects it and shows a white ON light.
  • Does the EXT PWR ON light go out when the engine generators take over the network? No, the ON light stays on even when the engine generators supply the aircraft. The engine generators have priority, but the ON light remains until the crew deselects external power.

Generators

  • When does a generator's line contactor close with the GEN pushbutton ON? When the generator's electrical parameters are normal. The generator control unit checks voltage and frequency before connecting the generator to its bus.
  • What does selecting a GEN pushbutton OFF do? Opens the line contactor, de-energises the generator and resets the fault circuit. GEN OFF is reversible, unlike the IDG disconnect.
  • What causes the GEN FAULT light? The generator control unit tripping the generator, or the line contactor opening with the pushbutton not OFF. FAULT shows a protection trip or an unexpected open contactor.
  • Besides normal parameters, what condition must be met for the APU generator line contactor to close? The external power line contactor must be open. Because external power has priority, the APU generator connects only when external power is not supplying.
  • When is the APU GEN FAULT light inhibited? When APU speed is too low, or when external power or an engine generator is providing power. In these cases an unconnected APU generator is normal, not a fault.

Distribution

  • What does the BUS TIE pushbutton in AUTO do? Opens or closes the bus tie contactors automatically to keep both AC BUS 1 and AC BUS 2 supplied. The contactors close as needed so a single source can feed both sides.
  • When are both bus tie contactors closed? In single-engine generator operation, or when the APU generator or external power supplies the network. With a single source, both sides must be tied to it.
  • What happens when the BUS TIE pushbutton is selected OFF? Both bus tie contactors open, so each AC bus is supplied only by its own engine generator. OFF isolates the two sides from each other.
  • From which bus is the AC ESS BUS normally supplied? AC BUS 1. AC ESS FEED in NORMAL supplies the AC ESS BUS from AC BUS 1; ALTN takes it from AC BUS 2.
  • If the normal AC ESS feed fails, what happens without crew action? The AC ESS BUS is automatically supplied from AC BUS 2 after 5 seconds. The automatic transfer happens regardless of the AC ESS FEED pushbutton position.
  • What does the AC ESS FEED FAULT light indicate? The AC ESS BUS is not electrically supplied. FAULT, with an ECAM caution, means the essential AC bus itself has no power.

Emergency

  • In case of total loss of the main generators, what supplies the AC ESS BUS? The emergency generator if available, otherwise the static inverter. The essential bus is kept alive first by the RAT-driven emergency generator, then by the batteries through the static inverter.
  • Which condition, together with the loss of AC BUS 1 and AC BUS 2, makes the RAT extend automatically? Speed above 100 kt. With both main AC buses lost above 100 kt, the RAT extends and the Blue system drives the emergency generator.
  • When is the emergency generator connected to the network after an automatic RAT extension? As soon as its electrical parameters are within tolerance. The generator is brought on line once its voltage and frequency are correct.
  • After a manual RAT extension with MAN ON, when is the emergency generator coupled? 3 seconds after the RAT is supplying the emergency generator. MAN ON selects manual RAT extension; coupling follows 3 s after the RAT drives the generator.
  • When does the red FAULT light on the EMER ELEC PWR panel come on? When the emergency generator is not supplying power while AC BUS 1 and AC BUS 2 are unpowered. It is red because the aircraft is then running on batteries alone.
  • During the EMER GEN TEST on the ground with normal AC buses, which buses are NOT powered by the emergency generator? The AC ESS SHED and DC ESS SHED buses. The test connects AC ESS and DC ESS to the emergency generator, driven by the Blue electric pump; the shed buses lose power.

IDG

  • What does pressing a guarded IDG pushbutton do? It disconnects the IDG from its drive shaft. The switches are spring-loaded out; pressing disconnects the drive mechanically.
  • Who can reconnect a disconnected IDG? Only maintenance personnel. The disconnection cannot be reversed from the cockpit.
  • Above what IDG oil outlet temperature does the IDG FAULT light come on? 185 °C. An IDG oil overheat above 185 °C, or low IDG oil pressure, gives the FAULT light.
  • When is the IDG low oil pressure fault inhibited? At low engine speed, with N2 below 14 %. A stopped or slow engine naturally has low IDG oil pressure, so the alert is inhibited.

Commercial loads

  • With GALY & CAB in AUTO, when is the cabin power shed? When only one generator is operating. One generator cannot comfortably carry the galleys; with the APU generator or external power all galleys are available.
  • When does the GALY & CAB FAULT light come on? When any generator's load exceeds 100 % of its rated output. The FAULT warns the crew that cabin loads are overloading the generators.
  • Which of these is switched off by the COMMERCIAL pushbutton? The water and toilet system. COMMERCIAL covers cabin and cargo lights, water and toilet, drain mast ice protection, galley, entertainment and cargo loading.

Smoke

  • What happens when GEN 1 LINE is selected OFF? GEN 1's line contactor opens; GEN 1 keeps running and supplies one fuel pump in each wing directly. AC BUS 1 is then supplied from GEN 2 through the bus ties.
  • What does the amber SMOKE light on the GEN 1 LINE pushbutton indicate? Smoke in the avionics ventilation duct. GEN 1 LINE is part of the smoke drill used to isolate an electrical smoke source.

Indications

  • On the ECAM ELEC page, when is the battery current shown in amber? When the battery is discharging at more than 5 A. A large discharge means the battery is carrying loads it should not.
  • On the ELEC page, what does an amber arrow pointing away from the battery mean? The contactor is closed and the battery is discharging at more than 1 A. A green arrow is charging above 1 A; amber is discharging above 1 A; no line is an open contactor.
  • When is the DC BAT voltage shown in amber on the ELEC page? Below 25 V. DC BAT below 25 V is amber.
  • What does an amber SHED next to a bus on the ELEC page mean? The AC or DC ESS SHED bus is in shed mode. Shedding removes the less essential loads from the essential network.
  • When is a TR's current shown in amber on the ELEC page? When it is below 5 A. A TR delivering almost no current is likely not supplying its bus.
  • Outside what voltage range is a generator's voltage shown in amber? 110 V to 120 V. Generator voltage is amber below 110 V or above 120 V.
  • Outside what frequency range is a generator's frequency shown in amber? 390 Hz to 410 Hz. Frequency is amber below 390 Hz or above 410 Hz.
  • When is a generator load shown in amber on the ELEC page? Above 100 %. Over 100 % load is beyond the rated output.
  • On the ELEC page, what does amber APU GEN with a white OFF show? The APU master switch is ON but the APU GEN pushbutton is off. The APU is running but its generator has been deselected.
  • Between what IDG oil outlet temperatures does the indication pulse on the ELEC page? 147 °C to 180 °C. It pulses above 147 °C as an advisory and turns amber above 180 °C.
  • What does an amber DISC under the IDG legend on the ELEC page mean? The IDG is disconnected. LO PR would mean low IDG oil pressure; DISC means the drive has been disconnected.

27 Flight Controls 87 questions

Seven computers, three laws, and a set of protections in normal law. The numbers here — bank, pitch, load factor, alpha floor, flap speeds — are the ones checkrides ask for. Sidestick priority is here too.

Computers

  • In normal operation, which computer commands the ailerons? ELAC 1. ELAC 1 normally drives the ailerons; ELAC 2 drives the elevators and stabiliser.
  • In normal operation, which computer commands the elevators and the horizontal stabiliser? ELAC 2. ELAC 2 has pitch; ELAC 1 has the ailerons. Each can take over from the other.
  • Which computers provide the aileron droop function? The ELACs. Aileron droop, normal and alternate pitch and roll, direct law and autopilot order acquisition are ELAC functions.
  • How many SECs (spoiler elevator computers) are there? Three. SEC 1, 2 and 3 command the spoilers.
  • Which SECs can provide alternate and direct pitch control? SEC 1 and SEC 2 only. SEC 3 controls spoilers only; SEC 1 and 2 also back up the elevators and stabiliser.
  • Which computers control the speed brakes and ground spoilers? The SECs. The SECs control the spoilers for roll, speed brake and ground spoiler functions.
  • Which of these is a FAC function? Wind shear detection. FACs handle rudder trim, rudder travel limit, yaw damping, alternate yaw, flight envelope and speed computations, and wind shear detection.
  • How is a flight control computer reset from the overhead panel? By selecting its pushbutton OFF then ON. Cycling the pushbutton OFF then ON resets the computer.
  • How long is a flight control computer's power-up test, during which its FAULT light is on? 8 seconds. The test runs at power application or after a transient; FAULT goes out when it completes satisfactorily.

Laws

  • Does the A320 remain in normal law after a single failure of a sensor, electrical system, hydraulic system or flight control computer? Yes, normal law is kept after a single failure. The redundancy is designed so that a single failure does not degrade the law.
  • Which failure mainly leads to direct law? Failure of all three IRs. Losing all three inertial references leaves no attitude data for the protected laws.
  • What controls remain in mechanical back-up after loss of all electrical power to the flight controls? The pitch trim wheel and the rudder pedals. The stabiliser and the rudder are mechanically controlled as a last resort.
  • Which failure leads to alternate law with reduced protection? Failure of both ELACs. Double ADR or IR failures, both ELACs, one elevator, one sidestick, all spoilers, all SECs or a THS jam all give alternate law.
  • Loss of the Green and Yellow hydraulic systems gives which law? Alternate law with reduced protection and mechanical yaw control. Without Green and Yellow, yaw falls back to the mechanical path.
  • Failure of all three ADRs gives which law? Alternate law without protection. Without air data, the speed-based protections cannot work.
  • In alternate law with the autopilot off, what happens to the law when the landing gear is extended? It changes to direct law. There is no flare mode in alternate law, so direct law gives the pilot a natural feel for landing.
  • In alternate law with reduced protection, which protection remains? Load factor protection. Load factor protection stays; α and overspeed protections are lost, replaced by speed stability.
  • In alternate law, what does speed stability do? It pushes the nose down near the stall warning speed and pulls it up at high speed, but can be overridden. It is not a protection: sidestick inputs overrule it.
  • In alternate law, how is roll controlled? In direct mode, without bank angle protection or turn coordination. Roll becomes direct; yaw damping is still available.
  • Above what bank angle does abnormal attitude law engage? 125°. Abnormal attitude law also engages beyond 50° pitch up, 30° pitch down or extreme speeds and angles of attack.
  • At what pitch attitudes does abnormal attitude law engage? Above 50° nose up or 30° nose down. These are far beyond the normal law limits of 30° up and 15° down.
  • After recovery from abnormal attitude law, what law remains? Pitch and yaw stay in alternate law for the rest of the flight. Flight controls stay degraded after an abnormal attitude.
  • Which PFD message tells the crew that the aircraft is in direct law? USE MAN PITCH TRIM in amber. Direct law has no auto trim, so the crew must trim manually.
  • In direct law, how is pitch controlled? Sidestick inputs move the elevators directly, with no auto trim. Auto trim and speed stability are lost.

Protections

  • At the latest, when does High Speed Protection become active? At VMO + 6 kt or MMO + 0.015. It can activate from VMO/MMO depending on conditions, and at the latest at VMO + 6 kt / MMO + 0.015.
  • At what speed does the autopilot disconnect automatically in high speed protection? VMO + 15 kt or MMO + 0.04. The AP disconnects at VMO + 15 kt / MMO + 0.04.
  • With full nose-down stick held in high speed protection, to about what speed is the aircraft limited? Around VMO + 16 kt and MMO + 0.04. Pilot nose-down authority is reduced so the speed is held near VMO + 16 kt.
  • In high speed protection, what happens to the bank angle when the sidestick is released? It returns to 0°. Outside HSP a released stick holds up to 33°; in HSP the aircraft rolls level.
  • To what is the bank angle limit reduced in high speed protection? 40°. The normal 67° limit drops to 40° in high speed protection.
  • What happens to the pitch trim in high speed protection? It is frozen. Auto trim stops so the nose-up correction is not trimmed out.
  • Which of these is part of the recommended recovery from high speed protection? Extend the speed brakes. Extend speed brakes, increase pitch and reduce thrust (or use A/THR).
  • Above 100 ft RA, when does the high angle of attack protection engage? When the angle of attack exceeds α prot. Below 100 ft RA on landing it engages when α max is reached.
  • Once α exceeds α prot, how does the angle of attack relate to the sidestick? The angle of attack becomes proportional to stick deflection, and auto trim stops. Neutral stick holds α prot; full aft stick gives α max.
  • Can a pilot stall an A320 in normal law by pulling the sidestick fully back? No, α max cannot be exceeded. Full aft stick holds α max, a small margin from the stall angle of attack.
  • To what is the bank angle limit reduced when high angle of attack protection is active? 45°. The 67° limit is reduced to 45° in high angle of attack protection.
  • What is VLS? The lowest selectable speed, giving an appropriate margin to the stall. The autopilot will not go below VLS with autothrust active.
  • What is green dot speed? The best lift-to-drag ratio speed in the clean configuration. Green dot is used as the engine-out and holding reference in the clean configuration.
  • What is the load factor limit in normal law in the clean configuration? –1.0 g to +2.5 g. Load factor protection lets the pilot pull full aft without overstressing the aircraft.
  • What is the load factor limit in normal law with slats or flaps extended? 0 g to +2.0 g. The limit is lower with high lift devices extended.
  • When does the G LOAD indication appear in amber on the lower ECAM? Above 1.4 g or below 0.7 g for more than 2 seconds. It appears on the system display as a crew awareness cue.
  • In CONF 0 to 3, what is the nose-up pitch attitude limit? 30°, reduced progressively to 25° at low speed. In CONF FULL the limit is 25°, reduced to 20° at low speed.
  • What is the nose-down pitch attitude limit? 15°. Pitch is limited to 15° nose down.
  • At what pitch attitudes do the flight director bars disappear? Above 25° up or 13° down. They return at 22° up or 10° down.
  • What is the maximum bank angle with the sidestick held fully laterally in normal law? 67°. 67° corresponds to the 2.5 g limit in a level turn.
  • In normal law the sidestick is released at 50° of bank. What happens? The bank reduces automatically to 33° and is held there. Up to 33° the bank is held with a neutral stick; above it, the aircraft rolls back to 33°.
  • Above what bank angle does the autopilot disconnect and the FD bars disappear? 45°. They return below 40°.
  • After take-off, up to what radio height is the reactive windshear warning available? 1 300 ft RA. It starts a few seconds after lift-off. On landing it is available from 1 300 ft RA down to 50 ft RA, with at least CONF 1.
  • How is a predictive or reactive windshear warning presented? A red WINDSHEAR message on both PFDs and the voice 'WINDSHEAR' three times. The crew sets TOGA and follows the SRS pitch order on the FD.
  • What is the low energy aural alert? 'SPEED SPEED SPEED'. It asks the crew to increase thrust to recover a positive flight path.
  • In which configurations is the low energy warning available? CONF 2, 3 and FULL. It is computed by the FAC from configuration, deceleration and flight path angle.
  • In which height band is the low energy warning available (outside it, it is inhibited)? Between 100 ft and 2 000 ft RA. It is inhibited below 100 ft RA and above 2 000 ft RA, among other conditions.

Alpha floor

  • What is α floor in CONF 0? 9.5°. α floor is 9.5° clean, 15° in CONF 1 and 2, 14° in CONF 3 and 13° in CONF FULL.
  • What is α floor in CONF FULL? 13°. It falls as flaps extend: 15° in CONF 1/2, 14° in CONF 3, 13° in CONF FULL.
  • When can alpha floor engage? From lift-off until 100 ft RA before landing. It is armed through the flight, but not in the last 100 ft before touchdown.
  • Besides α floor itself, what other trigger can activate alpha floor? Sidestick deflection above 14° with high angle of attack or pitch attitude protection active. A large aft stick input in a protection is treated as a sign the pilot needs thrust.
  • When is alpha floor inhibited? Above Mach 0.6, or with the TCAS mode engaged. TCAS manoeuvres and high Mach are excluded.
  • What does alpha floor do to the thrust? Sets TOGA thrust regardless of thrust lever position. Thrust lever positions are ignored when alpha floor is active.
  • How is alpha floor shown on the FMA? A.FLOOR with a flashing amber box. It appears in the first (autothrust) column.
  • After alpha floor, what does the FMA show when TOGA thrust remains locked? TOGA LK. TOGA LK stays until the crew disconnects autothrust, sets the levers and re-engages A/THR.

Indications

  • On the F/CTL page, when is a spoiler shown as a green triangle? When it is deflected more than 2.5°. A green line shows a retracted spoiler.
  • What does an amber PITCH TRIM legend on the F/CTL page mean? The pitch trim is jammed. A jammed THS is one of the causes of alternate law.
  • On the F/CTL page, what does an amber rudder travel limiter indication mean? Both rudder travel limiters have failed. The rudder travel limiter reduces rudder authority as speed increases.
  • On the F/CTL page, what does an amber ELAC or SEC number mean? That computer has failed or is switched OFF. Green numbers show available computers.

High lift

Tanks

  • What is the capacity of each of the two outer tanks on the A320? 1 760 litres (about 1 408 kg). Outer tanks are the small cells near the wing tips.
  • What is the capacity of each inner tank on the A320? 13 849 litres (about 11 079 kg). The inner tanks are the largest single tanks, one in each wing.
  • What is the capacity of the centre tank on the A320? 8 250 litres (about 6 600 kg). The centre tank sits in the wing box between the wings.

Sequence

  • Which tank is emptied first in normal operation? The centre tank. Keeping the wing tanks full longest relieves wing bending loads.
  • Down to what quantity are the inner tanks used before the outer tank fuel is transferred? 750 kg in each inner tank. At 750 kg in an inner tank, the outer tank fuel starts transferring into it.
  • How is outer tank fuel used? It is transferred into the inner tanks. Outer tanks have no engine feed of their own; their fuel drains into the inner tanks.

Crossfeed

  • What does the crossfeed capability allow? Both engines to be fed from one side, or one engine to be fed from both sides. Each engine is normally fed by its own side; the crossfeed valve links the two.
  • On the X FEED pushbutton, what does the green OPEN light indicate? The crossfeed valve is fully open. ON commands the valve open; the green OPEN light confirms it has reached fully open.

Pumps

  • In normal operation, what is done with the wing tank pumps during flight? They stay on throughout the flight. The wing pumps are left on; the system manages the feed sequence.
  • What does a wing tank pump pushbutton show when selected OFF? A white OFF light. OFF switches the pump off and lights white OFF.
  • When does a wing tank pump FAULT light come on? When the pump pressure drops and the pump is not selected OFF. FAULT, with an ECAM caution, is low delivery pressure on a pump that should be running.

Centre tank

  • On the A320neo, what has replaced the traditional centre tank pumps? Jet pumps, with only their valves controlled by the crew. The neo transfers centre tank fuel with jet pumps; the CTR TK pushbuttons control valves.
  • On the A320neo in AUTO, when do the centre-to-inner transfer valves open? When the inner tank is 500 kg below its capacity. Centre tank fuel tops up the inner tanks once they have used 500 kg.
  • On the A320neo in AUTO, when do the centre-to-inner transfer valves close? When the inner tank is full, or 5 minutes after the centre tank low level is reached. The 5-minute delay after the low level lets the last centre fuel be transferred.
  • What is the centre tank low fuel level used by the transfer logic? 130 kg. Transfer stops 5 minutes after the centre tank reaches 130 kg.
  • What does the fuel MODE SEL in MAN allow? Manual control of the centre tank transfer with the centre tank pushbuttons. In MAN, ON opens the centre tank valve and OFF closes it.
  • What triggers the fuel MODE SEL FAULT light? A wing tank below 5 000 kg while the centre tank still holds more than 250 kg. It indicates centre tank fuel is not being transferred as it should.
  • What does a CTR TK pushbutton show when selected OFF? A white OFF light, with the valve closed. OFF closes the centre tank valve.

Indications

  • On the FUEL page, what does an amber LO at a wing pump mean? The pump contactor is off. An amber crossline would show low pressure with the contactor on.
  • On the FUEL page, what does an amber crossline at an engine low-pressure valve mean? The valve is closed while the engine master switch is ON. Closed with the master ON is a disagreement that would starve the engine.
  • On the FUEL page, what does an amber inline crossfeed valve mean? The valve is open while the X FEED selector is off. Amber marks disagreement between valve and switch.
  • In what fuel temperature range are the FUEL page temperatures shown green without advisory? –40 °C to +45 °C. Outside this range the temperature becomes an advisory.
  • When is a tank's fuel temperature not displayed on the FUEL page? When the fuel temperature sensor is dry. No fuel at the sensor means no valid fuel temperature.
  • What do two dashes on the last digits of a tank quantity mean? The fuel quantity is inaccurate. The quantity is still shown but its last digits cannot be trusted.
  • What does an amber box around the fuel on board (FOB) mean? Some fuel is not usable, for example a failed centre transfer or a low inner tank level. Causes include a failed or off centre tank pump, transfer valves that cannot open, a low inner tank level or an unusable ACT.
  • When is an engine's fuel used figure shown in amber on the FUEL page? When the engine is below idle. Fuel used counts from engine start until electrical power off.
  • What does an amber XX in place of the total fuel flow mean? There is no fuel flow data. XX is the ECAM's way of saying 'no valid data'.

29 Hydraulic 28 questions

Green, Yellow and Blue, fully independent. Engine pumps drive Green and Yellow, an electric pump drives Blue, the PTU shares power between Green and Yellow, and the RAT drives Blue in an emergency.

Architecture

  • How many hydraulic systems does the A320 have, and how are they related? Three fully independent systems: Green, Yellow and Blue. Each system has its own reservoir and pumps; no fluid passes between them.

Pumps

  • What pressurises the Green system in normal operation? An engine-driven pump on engine 1. Green and Yellow each have one engine-driven pump; Green's is on engine 1.
  • What pressurises the Yellow system in normal operation? An engine-driven pump on engine 2. Yellow's engine-driven pump is on engine 2.
  • What pressurises the Blue system in normal operation? An electric pump. Blue has no engine-driven pump. The RAT is its emergency source.
  • What does selecting an ENG PUMP pushbutton OFF do? Deactivates (depressurises) the pump so it generates no hydraulic power. OFF depressurises the pump; the fire valve is closed by the engine fire pushbutton, not this one.
  • With the BLUE ELEC PUMP in AUTO and AC power available, when does the pump run? In flight, or on the ground with one engine running (or with BLUE PUMP OVRD pressed on the maintenance panel). The Blue pump does not run on the ground with both engines stopped unless overridden.
  • With the YELLOW ELEC PUMP OFF, what makes it start automatically? Setting the cargo door manual selector valve lever to OPEN or CLOSE. The pump starts to work the cargo door.
  • When the Yellow electric pump runs for the cargo doors, which Yellow functions remain available? Alternate braking and engine 2 reverse. Other Yellow functions are inhibited while the cargo door is being moved.

PTU

  • Engine 1 is not running (or the Green pump has failed). How can the Green system be pressurised? By the reversible PTU, from the Yellow system. The PTU uses Yellow pressure to drive a pump in the Green system.
  • With the PTU in AUTO, when does it run? When the differential pressure between Green and Yellow exceeds 500 psi. It is bidirectional and works towards whichever of the two is lower.
  • During the first engine start, what happens to the PTU? It is inhibited. The PTU is inhibited during the first engine start.
  • During the second engine start, what happens to the PTU? It is automatically tested. The second start is when the PTU is tested automatically.
  • What does selecting the PTU OFF do? Closes both PTU valves, stopping power transfer. OFF stops all transfer in both directions.
  • What causes a PTU FAULT light? A Green or Yellow reservoir overheat, low air pressure or low fluid level. The PTU is protected by watching the reservoirs of the two systems it links.

RAT

  • In a dual engine failure or total electrical power loss, which system does the RAT pressurise? Blue. The RAT drives a pump in the Blue system, which in turn can drive the emergency generator.
  • When can the crew extend the RAT with RAT MAN ON? At any time. RAT MAN ON extends it manually at any time; it extends automatically when AC BUS 1 and 2 are lost.

Ground

  • On the ground, which systems can be pressurised by electric pumps? Blue and Yellow. Green has no electric pump; on the ground it can be pressurised by the PTU from Yellow.
  • What is the Yellow system hand pump used for? Operating the cargo doors from the ground. The hand pump lets the cargo doors be moved without electrical power.

Faults

  • Which of these does NOT cause an ENG PUMP FAULT light? A PTU in operation. FAULT is caused by low reservoir level, reservoir overheat, low reservoir air pressure or low pump pressure.
  • When is the low pump pressure part of the ENG PUMP FAULT inhibited? On the ground with the engine stopped. A stopped engine's pump naturally has no pressure.
  • After a hydraulic overheat, does selecting the pump OFF extinguish the FAULT light? No, it stays on as long as the overheat lasts. Other faults go out when OFF is selected; an overheat FAULT remains until the overheat ends.

Indications

  • Below what system pressure is a hydraulic system shown in amber on the HYD page? 1 450 psi. Normal pressure is about 3 000 psi; below 1 450 psi the system is considered low.
  • On the HYD page, how is a closed hydraulic fire valve shown? Amber crossline. A closed fire valve cuts the fluid supply to that engine's pump.
  • What does an amber OVHT at a reservoir on the HYD page mean? The temperature of the returning fluid is above normal. Reservoir OVHT is about the return fluid; a separate OVHT next to an electric pump concerns that pump.
  • What does a green RAT arrow on the HYD page show? The RAT is deployed. White is stowed, green deployed, amber not available.
  • On the HYD page, green PTU arrows all pointing to the left (towards Green) mean what? The PTU is supplying the Green system. Arrows point to the system receiving power.
  • On the HYD page, what does an amber LO at an engine pump mean? The pump is ON but the hydraulic pressure is low. An amber crossline would instead show the pump selected OFF.
  • What does an amber arrow at the Yellow electric pump on the HYD page mean? The pump is ON and the Yellow pressure is low. White is off, green on and normal, amber on with low pressure.

30 Ice and Rain Protection 39 questions

Hot air for the outer slats and the engine intakes; electrical heat for windows, probes and the drain mast. Know what icing conditions are, and what each valve does when power fails.

Icing conditions

  • Icing conditions exist when the OAT (or TAT in flight) is at or below what value, with visible moisture? 10 °C. Visible moisture includes cloud, fog of 1 600 m visibility or less, rain, snow, sleet and ice crystals.
  • In the definition of icing conditions, fog counts as visible moisture when the visibility is at or below what? 1 600 m (1 SM). Fog of 1 600 m or less is visible moisture for icing purposes.
  • On the ground at or below 10 °C OAT, which surface condition also counts as icing conditions, even without visible moisture in the air? Snow, standing water or slush on ramps, taxiways or runways that may be ingested or freeze on the engines. Contaminants can be thrown up into the engines and freeze on nacelles or probes.

Wing anti-ice

  • Which areas are protected by hot air? The three outboard slats of each wing and the engine air intakes. Windows, sensors, probes and the drain mast are heated electrically.
  • Which slats are heated by wing anti-ice? Slats 3, 4 and 5 of each wing. Only the three outboard slats are protected.
  • How is hot air supplied to the slats of each wing? Through one valve in each wing. The WING pushbutton controls both valves.
  • What happens when WING anti-ice is selected ON on the ground? A 30-second test sequence is run. Continuous heating without airflow would overheat the slats.
  • What happens if a leak is detected while wing anti-ice is in use? The affected side's wing anti-ice valve closes automatically. Only the leaking side is isolated.
  • What does selecting wing anti-ice do to the engine limits? The N1 (or EPR) limit is reduced and the idle N1 (or EPR) is increased. The extra bleed demand needs more idle and leaves less maximum thrust.
  • If the electrical supply fails, what do the wing anti-ice valves do? They close. This is the opposite of the engine anti-ice valves, which open on electrical failure.
  • Which ECAM memo appears when WING anti-ice is ON? WING A. ICE. The pushbutton shows a blue ON light.
  • What causes the WING anti-ice FAULT light? A valve not in the required position, or low pressure detected. FAULT comes with an ECAM caution.
  • In flight, when must wing anti-ice be ON? When there is visible airframe icing on the visual ice indicator or the wipers. Wing anti-ice is used when ice is actually building, not just in icing conditions.

Probes and windows

  • Which of these is protected by electrical heating? The waste-water drain mast. Electrical heating protects the cockpit windows, the sensors, pitot probes, static ports and the drain mast.
  • How many Window Heat Computers are there? Two, one on each side. They regulate, protect against overheat and indicate faults.
  • How does windshield heating operate on the ground? At low power, changing automatically to normal power in flight. The side windows have only one heating level.
  • How many Probe Heating Computers are there? Three: Captain, F/O and standby probes. They control and monitor the probes and protect against overheat.
  • Which sensors does the probe heating system protect? Pitot heads, static ports, angle of attack probes and TAT probes. All four kinds of sensor are electrically heated (TAT not on the ground).
  • On the ground, how are the TAT probes and the pitot heads heated? TAT probes are not heated and pitot heating is at low level. Changeover to normal power is automatic.
  • With PROBE/WINDOW HEAT in AUTO, when are the probes and windows heated? When at least one engine is running, or in flight. ON (blue light) heats them permanently, for example before engine start.

Engine anti-ice

  • Where does the engine anti-ice air come from? An independent bleed from the high-pressure compressor of each engine. Each nacelle has its own independent supply.
  • What type of valve supplies engine anti-ice air? A two-position (open or closed) valve, one per engine. Each is controlled by its own ENG pushbutton.
  • What does the engine anti-ice valve do when no air is available (engine not running)? It closes automatically. Without pressure the valve closes.
  • If electrical power fails, what do the engine anti-ice valves do? They open. Failing open ensures the intakes stay protected.
  • When an engine anti-ice valve opens, which engines have their idle increased if necessary? Both engines. Both idles may rise to give the required bleed pressure.
  • When engine anti-ice is ON and its valve is open, what is selected automatically? Continuous ignition, with the IGNITION memo. Continuous ignition protects against flame-out from ice ingestion.
  • What causes the ENG anti-ice FAULT light? The valve position disagreeing with the pushbutton selection. FAULT comes with an ECAM caution.
  • On the ground, when must engine anti-ice be ON? During all ground operations where icing conditions exist or are anticipated. Engines are vulnerable to ice ingestion on the ground.
  • In which flight case is engine anti-ice not required even in icing conditions? In the climb or cruise when the SAT is below –40 °C. Very cold air holds ice crystals that do not stick.

Rain removal

  • On what kind of windshield must the wipers never be used? A dry windshield. Running wipers dry scratches the glass.
  • How many speeds do the A320's windshield wipers have? Two (SLOW and FAST). Each side has its own rotary selector; OFF stows the wiper at the base of the window.
  • After applying rain repellent, how long does it take for the windows to be covered with the spray? About 30 seconds. Repellent is used in moderate to heavy rain only.
  • When is rain repellent inhibited? On the ground with the engines not running. Each press applies a measured quantity; press again to repeat.
  • Which use of rain repellent is prohibited? On a dry windshield, or to wash the windshield. On a dry windshield it smears and reduces visibility.

Ice detection

  • How many ice detector probes does the ice detection system have, and where are they? Two, on the forward lower fuselage. They detect ice accretion and generate ECAM messages.
  • Does the ice detection system switch the engine or wing anti-ice on automatically? No, it does not control them. It only advises the crew through the ECAM.
  • Where is the external visual ice indicator? Between the two windshields. The crew uses it to judge visible airframe icing.

Procedures

  • When taxiing in icing conditions, when should the flaps be extended? At the runway holding point. Delaying extension keeps contamination out of the mechanism.
  • After landing from an approach in icing conditions, when may the flaps be retracted? After the ground crew confirms the flaps and slats are clear of ice. Retracting with ice can damage the mechanism.

The FADEC starts, controls and limits each engine. Thrust levers sit in detents that set limits; the E/WD shows what each engine is doing. Engine starting with bleed air from the other engine or a ground unit is here too.

Engine start

  • When is a crossbleed engine start used? When a normal APU bleed start is not possible, usually because the APU is inoperative. Air from the running engine is used to start the second engine.
  • In a crossbleed start, why is the receiving engine's ENG BLEED selected OFF? To prevent the running engine's air escaping back through the receiving engine. With the bleed valve closed, the air goes to the start valve instead of leaking out through the bleed duct.
  • Where is the X BLEED selector set for a crossbleed engine start? OPEN. The cross bleed valve must be open to join the running engine's duct to the other side.
  • What minimum bleed pressure is required before attempting a start from a crossbleed or an air start unit? 30 psi. If the pressure is lower, the running engine's thrust is increased (or the ASU checked) before starting.
  • During a crossbleed start, what pressure should be maintained throughout? At least 25 psi. Pressure often drops as the start valve opens; thrust is added on the supplying engine to keep at least 25 psi.
  • During a crossbleed start, the supplying engine needs about 40 % N1 to keep the pressure. What should the crew consider? Checking the area behind is clear and, if more than 40 % is needed, aborting as the configuration may be wrong. Needing much power suggests a leak or wrong valve setting, and jet blast becomes a hazard to people behind.
  • After a crossbleed start, what happens to the X BLEED selector? It goes back to AUTO. After both engines run: supplying engine to idle, X BLEED to AUTO, receiving ENG BLEED back ON.
  • When should a crossbleed engine start not be performed? During pushback, or where the engine's thrust would endanger people or property. The supplying engine needs significant thrust, which is dangerous behind the aircraft.
  • Why is external electrical power required for an air start unit (ASU) start? Because the inoperative APU is not supplying electrical power. The ASU supplies air only; electrical power has to come from a ground power unit.
  • Before an ASU is connected, how are the packs and the X BLEED selector set? Both packs OFF and X BLEED OPEN, with all bleeds OFF. All the ASU's air must go to the starter, so nothing else draws on it.

Start

  • In which position is the ENG MODE selector for normal operation? NORM. IGN/START is used for starting on the ground and continuous ignition in flight.
  • What does selecting ENG MODE to IGN/START do in flight? It provides continuous ignition as required. On the ground it starts the automatic or manual start sequence.
  • With ENG MODE at CRANK and ENG MAN START ON, what happens? The start valve opens but the ignition does not fire. Cranking turns the engine with the starter only.
  • What does setting an ENG MASTER lever to ON do? Tells the FADEC to start the automatic or manual start sequence. The start sequence follows the ENG MAN START pushbutton position.
  • What does setting an ENG MASTER lever to OFF do? Shuts the engine down or aborts its start sequence. It is the normal shutdown control.
  • Which condition turns on the FAULT light on the pedestal ENG panel? An automatic start sequence being aborted. FAULT also shows an HP fuel shut-off valve in an abnormal position or a thrust control malfunction. FIRE has its own light.
  • With ENG MODE at CRANK, ENG MASTER ON and ENG MAN START ON, what process takes place? A wet crank. Master ON adds fuel, without ignition — a wet crank.
  • With ENG MODE at CRANK, ENG MASTER OFF and ENG MAN START ON, what process takes place? A dry crank. No fuel and no ignition: the starter just turns the engine.

Thrust control

  • On which engines are the N1 MODE pushbuttons fitted? IAE V2500 engines (on the A320ceo). N1 mode switches thrust control from EPR to N1 in an abnormal situation.
  • In N1 unrated (degraded) mode, what protection is lost? The thrust lever detents no longer protect the limits, so overboost is possible. N1 then follows thrust lever position only.
  • How many FADECs control each engine? One FADEC dedicated to each engine. Each FADEC controls and limits its own engine.
  • Does the autothrust move the A320's thrust levers? No, they can only be moved manually. The levers set a limit; the A/THR controls the thrust within it.
  • If a thrust lever is placed between two detents, which rating limit does the FADEC select? The rating of the higher detent. The FADEC always takes the next detent up as the limit.
  • For a FLEX take-off thrust to be effective, the FLEX temperature must be higher than what? The current total air temperature (TAT). Without a valid FLEX temperature, the FLX/MCT detent gives MCT on the ground.
  • On the ground, with no valid FLEX temperature set, what thrust does the FLX/MCT detent give? Maximum continuous thrust (MCT). A FLEX temperature is needed for reduced take-off thrust.
  • After a FLEX take-off, can the crew return to FLEX thrust once they have moved the lever to TOGA or CL? No; FLX/MCT then gives MCT. Moving the lever out of FLX/MCT without reaching TOGA or CL has no effect.
  • How can MAX take-off thrust always be obtained? By pushing the thrust levers fully forward. TOGA is always available to the pilot.

Autothrust

  • What happens if one A/THR instinctive disconnect pushbutton is held for more than 15 seconds? The A/THR is disconnected for the rest of the flight, including alpha floor. It can only be recovered at the next FMGC power-up on the ground.
  • In autothrust, what limits the thrust the FMGC can command (outside alpha floor)? The thrust lever position. The levers set the maximum; alpha floor is the exception.

Indications

  • On the ENG page, when is the fuel filter clog indication displayed? When the pressure loss across the fuel filter is excessive. It is hidden when the filter pressure is normal.
  • When does the oil quantity pulse on the ENG page? When it is below the oil advisory limit. Pulsing is an advisory, before any caution.
  • What does a red oil pressure value on the ENG page mean? The oil pressure is in the red range. Amber is the amber range; red is the red (limit) range.
  • When are the N1 and N2 vibration values shown in amber on the ENG page? When the rotor vibration is excessive. Vibration is an advisory parameter for each rotor.
  • What does A FLOOR on the E/WD indicate? The alpha floor protection is active. It appears near the thrust rating display.
  • Which thrust rating is shown on the E/WD as MREV? Maximum reverse thrust. Other ratings include TOGA, MCT, CLB, FLX, derated take-off and GA SOFT.
  • What thrust rating does GA SOFT on the E/WD refer to? A soft go-around thrust. A soft go-around uses less than full TOGA thrust.
  • When is IDLE shown on the E/WD? When both engines are at idle in flight. It helps the crew notice that thrust is at idle in the descent.
  • What does an amber REV on the E/WD mean? The thrust reverser is unlocked. Green REV means fully deployed.
  • On the A320neo E/WD, above what N1 is the value shown in red? 104 %. Exceeding the N1 limit is amber; beyond the 104 % red line it is red.
  • What does the blue circle on the N1 gauge show? The thrust lever position. The amber mark is the N1 limit.
  • When is the N2 value shown in a grey box on the E/WD? When the engine is in its start sequence or being cranked. The grey box disappears once the engine is started.
  • On the A320neo E/WD, above what N2 is the value shown in red? 100 %. N2 has a red limit of 100 %.

PRO Autoland and Rejected Take-off 26 questions

Autoland limits and minima, the FMA sequence down to roll-out, and the speed bands that shape a rejected take-off decision.

Autoland

  • Which FMA approach capability means autoland is not authorised? CAT 1. An autoland needs at least CAT 2 shown on the FMA.

Autoland limits

  • What is the maximum crosswind for an autoland? 20 kt. Headwind is limited to 30 kt and tailwind to 10 kt.
  • What is the maximum headwind for an autoland? 30 kt. Crosswind 20 kt, tailwind 10 kt.
  • What is the maximum tailwind for an autoland? 10 kt. Tailwind is the most restrictive component.
  • Within what glideslope angle range is autoland permitted? –2.5° to –3.25°. Steeper or shallower glideslopes are outside the certified envelope.
  • Above what airport elevation is autoland not permitted? 9 200 ft. It is also not allowed below –2 000 ft pressure altitude.
  • Below what aircraft weight is autoland not permitted? 44 000 kg. It is also not permitted above the maximum landing weight.

Autoland minima

  • What is the minimum for an ILS CAT II approach? 100 ft decision height. CAT III SINGLE is 50 ft; CAT III DUAL may have no DH (RVR 75 m minimum).
  • What is the minimum RVR for a CAT III DUAL approach with no decision height? 75 m. No DH is possible with fail-operational capability.

Autoland sequence

  • At what height must LAND be shown on the FMA during an autoland? By 350 ft. If LAND is not shown, a go-around is flown (or a manual landing on a CAT II approach at or above 80 ft with visual references).
  • At what height should FLARE appear on the FMA during an autoland? 40 ft. Without FLARE at 40 ft, the crew goes around (or lands manually with visual references).
  • At about what height does the autothrust go to THR IDLE during an autoland? 30 ft. The 'RETARD' callout at 10 ft prompts the PF to set the levers to idle.
  • When does ROLL OUT appear on the FMA? At main gear touchdown. The autopilot keeps the aircraft on the centreline.
  • During an autoland, when does the aircraft remove its crosswind crab angle? Between about 20 ft and 10 ft. It aligns the longitudinal axis with the runway.
  • What does a triple click during an autoland approach indicate? A downgrade of landing capability. Below 1 000 ft AAL and above 100 ft RA a downgrade calls for a go-around.
  • On a CAT II approach, at or above what height must a takeover for a manual landing be made after an autoland problem? 80 ft. Visual references must be acquired.
  • Below 1 000 ft AAL during an autoland, which event allows the approach to continue rather than requiring a go-around? An engine fire warning. Fire and smoke warnings are handled after landing; AP loss, cautions and downgrades lead to a go-around.
  • Which of these triggers the AUTOLAND warning light in addition to signal and deviation problems? A long flare detected by the FMGS. With LAND or FLARE on the FMA and an AP engaged below 200 ft.

Go-around

  • On a go-around, towards what pitch attitude is the aircraft rotated? 15° nose up. Thrust to TOGA, rotate, and call 'Go around' together.
  • During a go-around, how is GA SOFT thrust obtained? By setting TOGA first (for SRS GA) and then FLX/MCT. The FMA then shows MAN GA SOFT.

Rejected take-off

  • If a take-off is rejected below 72 kt, what does not activate? The ground spoilers and the autobrake. Full manual braking is then required.
  • Into which speed phases is the take-off divided for the reject decision? Below 100 kt, between 100 kt and V1, and above V1. Above 100 kt rejecting becomes hazardous, so fewer reasons justify it.
  • Who makes the decision to reject the take-off? The captain. The captain keeps a hand on the thrust levers until V1 and calls 'STOP'.
  • From what speed does the ECAM inhibit non-essential warnings during the take-off? 80 kt. A magenta T.O INHIBIT memo shows on the E/WD.
  • When does the take-off inhibition of ECAM warnings end? At 1 500 ft, or 2 minutes after lift-off, whichever is first. Non-essential alerts are delayed until the critical phase is over.
  • Between 100 kt and V1, which amber caution is a reason to reject the take-off? ENG FAIL. Others include F/CTL SIDESTICK FAULT, ENG REVERSE FAULT or UNLOCKED, and THR LEVER FAULT.
