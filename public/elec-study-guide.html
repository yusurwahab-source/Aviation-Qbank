🔋 DC Principles 26 questions

Every electrical question rests on three quantities — voltage, current and resistance — and on knowing how they behave in series and in parallel.

🎯 Ohm's law and power🎯 Series vs parallel🎯 EMF vs terminal voltage🎯 Open and short circuits
💡 Key idea

Voltage pushes, resistance resists, current is the result. Voltage is energy per coulomb (J/C), current is coulombs per second (A), and their product is energy per second — power, in watts.

In series, one break stops everything and the supply voltage divides. In parallel, each branch sees the full voltage and works on its own.

🔗 Series

Same current everywhere. Rtotal = R₁ + R₂ + … Voltage divides in proportion to resistance. One open circuit stops the lot.

🪜 Parallel

Same voltage across each branch. Rtotal is less than the smallest branch. Adding a load lowers total resistance and raises total current.

EMF is not what you measure under load

A battery's EMF is its open-circuit voltage. Under load, part of it is lost driving current through its own internal resistance: Vterminal = EMF − I × r. That is why the bus voltage dips during a start, and why a weak battery can look healthy until it is asked for current.

FaultResistanceCurrentWhat you see
Open circuit∞0Load dead; full supply voltage across the break
Short circuit≈ 0Very highWiring overheats until protection opens it
🧠 Memory aid Metals get more resistive when hot (positive coefficient); semiconductors and carbon get less (negative coefficient). R = ρL/A: longer is worse, thicker is better.

🔀 Switches, Relays & Solenoids 20 questions

A light switch on the flight deck can command hundreds of amps somewhere in the aircraft. The trick is that it never carries them.

🎯 Poles and throws🎯 Relay vs solenoid🎯 Normally open / closed🎯 Arcing on inductive loads
💡 Key idea

Small current controls big current. The cockpit switch energises a coil; the coil's magnetic pull closes heavy contacts close to the load. Thin wires to the cockpit, short thick cables where the current flows.

SPST / SPDT / DPDT
Poles = separate circuits switched; throws = positions each pole can connect to.
Break-before-make
Old path opens before the new one closes — two sources are never joined, even for an instant.
Microswitch
Tiny movement, positive snap action. Position sensing for flaps, doors, gear, throttles.
Proximity switch
No contacts to wear or ice up; senses a target by its effect on a field.

🧲 Relay

Hinged armature, moderate currents. Can be normally open or closed, latching, polarised or time-delayed.

🔩 Solenoid (contactor)

Soft-iron plunger pulled into the coil — a long, strong stroke for battery, ground power and starter contactors.

Coil pull depends on ampere-turns. A low bus voltage means less current, fewer ampere-turns, and a relay that may not pull in or may chatter. Switching off a coil or a motor makes its collapsing field induce a voltage spike — hence suppression diodes across DC coils and de-rated contacts for inductive loads.

✈️ Pilot tip Switches go in the supply (positive) lead. With the switch off, everything downstream is dead — a chafed wire there cannot short to structure.

🛡️ Circuit Protection & Capacitors 24 questions

Protection exists for the wiring. A capacitor is the component that stores charge — and sometimes keeps it after everything is switched off.

🎯 Fuse vs breaker vs limiter🎯 Trip-free🎯 Reset policy🎯 Capacitors in series/parallel🎯 Time constant
💡 Key idea

The protection is sized to the cable, not to the load. Fitting a bigger fuse moves the weak link into the wiring behind the panel — where a fire starts unseen.

⚠️ Resetting breakers Trip-free breakers cannot be held in against a fault. In flight, reset once only, after a cooling period, and only if the system is needed — never for fuel-system circuits unless the checklist says so. A collar on a breaker means it was pulled on purpose.

🪫 Batteries 26 questions

The battery starts things and stands by. In normal flight the generators carry the load — the battery's real job begins when they stop.

🎯 Ah and endurance🎯 Lead-acid vs NiCd🎯 Specific gravity🎯 Thermal runaway🎯 Hot battery bus
💡 Key idea

Ampere-hours ÷ amps = hours — on a good day. Cold, a partial charge and heavy current all cut what a battery can really deliver. Plan on less, and shed load early.

ElectrolyteDilute sulphuric acidPotassium hydroxide (alkali)

Schematic shapes, not figures. A NiCd's steady voltage is useful but gives little warning that it is nearly flat.
🔥 Thermal runaway (NiCd, and lithium) Hot cell → lower internal resistance → more charge current → hotter still. A battery-temperature warning with a high charge rate means take it off charge as the checklist directs.
Series
Voltages add, capacity stays the same.
Parallel
Capacity adds, voltage stays the same.
Hot battery bus
Wired straight to the battery — live with the switch off. Why parked aircraft slowly go flat.
After a start
High charge current that falls away is normal. One that stays high is not.
✈️ Pilot tip Never let lead-acid and NiCd share tools or hydrometers — acid traces destroy alkaline cells.

🧲 Magnetism & Electromagnetism 22 questions

Generators, motors, relays and transformers are all the same two facts put to work: current makes a field, and a changing field makes a voltage.

🎯 Soft iron vs hard steel🎯 Fleming's rules🎯 Faraday and Lenz🎯 Eddy currents and laminations
💡 Key idea

Only change induces. A steady flux produces nothing; a changing one induces an EMF proportional to its rate of change (Faraday), in the direction that opposes the change (Lenz).

🧲 Soft iron

High permeability, low retentivity: magnetises easily and lets go when the current stops. Relay cores, electromagnets, transformer cores.

🔒 Hard steel / alloys

High retentivity and coercivity: keeps its magnetism. Permanent magnets — including the PMG in a brushless generator.

RuleUsed forFingers
Right-hand gripField around a conductor / polarity of a coilThumb = current (or N pole of coil)
Fleming's left handMotors — force on a conductorFirst = Field, seCond = Current, thuMb = Motion
Fleming's right handGenerators — induced currentFirst = Field, thuMb = Motion, seCond = induced Current

Force on a conductor is F = BIL. Induced EMF is greatest when a conductor cuts flux at right angles and zero when it moves along it. Parallel conductors attract when their currents flow the same way.

Reluctance
Opposition to flux. Air gaps add a lot of it.
Self-induction
A coil opposes changes in its own current — slow rise, spike at switch-off.
Mutual induction
One coil's changing flux induces a voltage in another — the transformer.
Core losses
Eddy currents (cut by laminating) and hysteresis (cut by soft magnetic material).
🧠 Memory aid Left for Lifting (motors push), Right for geneRating. Curie point: heat it enough and the magnet forgets.

⚙️ DC Generators & Alternators 24 questions

Light aircraft and many turboprops make DC. Engine speed changes all the time; the bus voltage must not.

🎯 Commutator🎯 Voltage regulation🎯 Reverse current cut-out🎯 Alternator needs the battery🎯 Load sharing
💡 Key idea

Output depends on speed × field; the regulator only controls the field. Faster engine → less field current. Slower engine → more field current, until even full field is not enough below cut-in speed.

  1. Armature coils always generate alternating EMF.
  2. Commutator and brushes reverse each coil's connection as its EMF reverses, giving DC at the brushes.
  3. Shunt field (in parallel with the armature) is fed through the voltage regulator — carbon pile or electronic.
  4. Reverse current cut-out connects the generator only once its voltage exceeds the bus, and opens if current flows back.

⚙️ DC generator

Self-excited from residual magnetism (lost it? flash the field). Needs an RCCO. Little output at idle.

🔆 Alternator

Rotating field via slip rings, stator output rectified by built-in diodes — the diodes block reverse current, so no cut-out. Needs battery voltage to excite: a flat battery means no output.

ItemWhy it matters
Regulated at ≈ 28 V (14 V)Must exceed the 24 V (12 V) battery's EMF to charge it
Overvoltage relayA failed regulator at full field would drive the bus far too high — trip the field
Equalising circuitParalleled DC generators share load by trimming each one's field
Armature reactionArmature current distorts the field — corrected by interpoles
Starter-generatorOne machine: motor for the start, generator once running
✈️ Pilot tip Very bright lights and a big positive ammeter reading together mean overvoltage — take the alternator off line.

🔌 DC Distribution & Indication 24 questions

Busbars decide which loads survive a failure. Meters tell you which source is actually carrying them.

🎯 Earth return vs two-wire🎯 Essential and battery buses🎯 Ammeter vs loadmeter🎯 External power interlocks
💡 Key idea

Group loads by importance, feed the important ones from more than one source. Then losing a source means shedding the bottom of the list, not the top.

🛩️ Earth return (single-pole)

The metal structure is the return conductor — one wire per load, much less weight. A chafed supply wire touching structure is a short.

➰ Two-wire (double-pole)

Supply and return side by side: their fields cancel near the compass, and composite structures that cannot carry the return need it.

Blocking diodes

Short control pin

Reverse polarity

Avionics master
Radios off during the start, away from voltage spikes.
✈️ Pilot tip A bus voltmeter reading about 24 V in flight on a 28 V system means the battery, not the generator, is carrying the load.

🔄 Motors & Actuators 24 questions

A motor is a generator run backwards — and the voltage it generates while spinning is what keeps its current in check.

🎯 Back-EMF🎯 Series vs shunt🎯 Reversing🎯 Induction motor slip🎯 Phase loss
💡 Key idea

I = (V − Eback) ÷ R. At standstill there is no back-EMF, so starting current is huge. As the motor speeds up, back-EMF rises and the current falls.

Reverse a DC motor
Reverse armature or field current — not both.
Reverse a 3-phase motor
Swap any two phases.
Synchronous speed

Lost a phase
Keeps running hot, will not restart. Thermal cut-outs protect it.

Actuators stop at limit switches, are held by a spring-applied, solenoid-released brake, and are protected by a slipping clutch if the output jams.

〰️ AC Principles 26 questions

Large aircraft run on 115/200 V, 400 Hz, three-phase AC. Once current alternates, inductance and capacitance join resistance in deciding how much flows.

🎯 RMS and peak🎯 ELI the ICE man🎯 Impedance🎯 Power factor and kVA🎯 Star and delta
💡 Key idea

RMS is the DC value with the same heating effect. Every AC figure on an aircraft is RMS unless stated: 115 V RMS swings to about ±163 V peak.

In a pure inductance the current (dashed) peaks a quarter cycle after the voltage. In a pure capacitance it peaks a quarter cycle before.
ElementOppositionWith frequencyCurrent vs voltage
ResistorRUnchangedIn phase
InductorXL = 2πfLRisesLags 90° (ELI)
CapacitorXC = 1/2πfCFallsLeads 90° (ICE)

Impedance Z = √(R² + (XL − XC)²): 30 Ω with 40 Ω reactance is 50 Ω, not 70. At series resonance XL = XC, so Z = R and current is at its maximum.

⚡ Power factor

PF = cos φ = W ÷ VA. Only in-phase current does work. A pure reactance takes no true power — the energy goes back and forth.

🏷️ Why kVA

Windings heat according to total current, whatever the PF. A 90 kVA generator at PF 0.8 gives at most 72 kW.

Three-phase

Line voltage√3 × phase (115 → 200 V)= phase

Power = √3 × VL × IL × PF. A balanced star load puts zero current in the neutral. 400 Hz is used because transformers and machines get much smaller and lighter; the price is higher reactance, which short aircraft cable runs can live with.

🌀 AC Generators 26 questions

Two jobs, two controls: hold the frequency by holding the speed, hold the voltage by controlling the field.

🎯 f = rpm × pairs ÷ 60🎯 Brushless three-stage🎯 CSD and IDG🎯 Paralleling conditions🎯 kW vs kVAR sharing
💡 Key idea

Speed sets frequency; excitation sets voltage. Every AC generator fault question becomes easy once you ask which of the two is wrong.

The GCU controls the stationary exciter field — the only adjustment needed to regulate the output voltage.
CSD
Hydro-mechanical drive: variable engine speed in, constant generator speed out.
IDG
CSD + generator in one oil-cooled unit. Disconnect in flight = reconnect only on the ground.
VSCF
No CSD: electronics turn variable-frequency output into 400 Hz.
Variable / wild frequency
Frequency follows engine speed. Fine for heaters and TRUs; cannot be paralleled.

Paralleling

  1. Match voltage, frequency, phase sequence and phase angle before closing the breaker.
  2. Share real load (kW) by adjusting each drive's speed — torque.
  3. Share reactive load (kVAR) by adjusting each generator's excitation.
ProtectionDetects
DifferentialCurrent leaking out between two sets of current transformers — a feeder or winding fault
Over/under-voltageRegulator or excitation failure
Over/under-frequencyCSD/IDG not holding speed
✈️ Pilot tip GCR controls the excitation; GCB connects the output to the bus. A serious fault opens both.

🏗️ AC Power Systems 24 questions

How generators, ground power, the APU, the RAT and the battery are arranged so that any one failure leaves the essentials powered.

🎯 Split vs parallel🎯 Bus tie logic🎯 External power checks🎯 RAT and inverter🎯 Load shedding
💡 Key idea

One source per bus, with automatic transfer. In a split system generators are never paralleled; bus ties close only to let a healthy source pick up a dead bus.

Schematic only. In a parallel system all generators instead feed one tie bus and share the load.

✂️ Split-bus

No frequency matching needed; a fault stays on its own side. A failure causes a brief transfer.

🤝 Parallel

Load shared, no break when one generator fails — but tight frequency and load control, and a fault is seen by all.

SourceRole
External powerChecked for voltage, frequency and phase sequence; not paralleled with the aircraft generators. Short pins E/F energise its contactor.
APU generatorConstant speed by itself; ground power and a backup in flight within its limits.
RATAirflow-driven emergency generator or hydraulic pump — essential loads only.
Static inverterBattery DC → essential AC when all generation is lost.
TRUsAC → 28 V DC for the DC buses.
ℹ️ Remember A bus tie lockout stops a healthy generator being connected to a faulted bus, and no-break power transfer overlaps two sources briefly so the buses never blink.

🔁 Transformers, TRUs & Inverters 22 questions

Changing voltage needs AC; changing between AC and DC needs rectifiers one way and inverters the other.

🎯 Turns ratio🎯 No DC through a transformer🎯 Current transformers🎯 Half- vs full-wave🎯 Static inverter
💡 Key idea

Power in ≈ power out. Step the voltage down by ten and the current steps up by ten. Vs/Vp = Ns/Np.

Double-wound
Separate windings, electrically isolated.
Autotransformer
One tapped winding — lighter, no isolation.
Current transformer
The feeder is its primary. Never open-circuit the secondary under load — dangerous voltage.
Losses
Copper (I²R) in windings; iron (eddy + hysteresis) in the core.
Full-wave rectification doubles the ripple frequency (800 Hz from a 400 Hz single-phase supply); a smoothing capacitor fills in the dips.

➡️ TRU

115/200 V AC → transformer → 6-diode bridge → ≈ 28 V DC. Two TRUs + a DC tie give redundancy.

⬅️ Static inverter

DC → oscillator-timed transistor switching through a transformer → 115 V / 26 V AC. No moving parts.

✈️ Pilot tip 400 Hz lets a transformer get by with far less iron than one for 50 Hz — the whole reason aircraft use it.

💎 Semiconductors 22 questions

Everything from the TRU's rectifier to the warning logic is built from doped silicon junctions.

🎯 N-type and P-type🎯 Forward and reverse bias🎯 Zener🎯 Transistor gain🎯 Thyristor latching
💡 Key idea

A PN junction is a one-way valve. Forward biased (P positive) it conducts after about 0.6–0.7 V for silicon; reverse biased it blocks until breakdown.

N-type

Five-valence donor (phosphorus, arsenic). Majority carriers: electrons.

P-type

Three-valence acceptor (boron, gallium). Majority carriers: holes.

DeviceWorks byUsed for
DiodeConducts one wayRectifiers, blocking, suppression

LED / photodiodeLight out when forward biased / leakage up with light inIndicators / light sensing

Thyristor (SCR)Gate pulse latches it on until current falls below holding valuePower control
NTC thermistorResistance falls as temperature risesTemperature sensing
⚠️ Heat and static Semiconductors conduct more when hot, so they need heat sinks to avoid thermal runaway — and body static can destroy them, hence ESD precautions.

🔢 Logic Circuits 20 questions

Warnings and system logic are decisions made from yes/no inputs. Six gates cover almost all of it.

🎯 Truth tables🎯 NAND and NOR🎯 XOR🎯 Real warning logic
💡 Key idea

AND needs all, OR needs any, a bubble inverts. NAND is 0 only when all inputs are 1; NOR is 1 only when all inputs are 0.

🌩️ Bonding, Screening & Static 20 questions

Make the aircraft one conductor, let static leave quietly, and keep stray fields out of the wiring.

🎯 Why bond🎯 Static wicks🎯 Refuelling🎯 Lightning paths🎯 Screening and HIRF
💡 Key idea

Parts at the same potential cannot spark at each other. Bonding gives every metal part a milliohm-level path to the rest — for static, for earth-return current and for lightning.

Composites
Need mesh or foil — they do not carry strike current well.
HIRF / EMC
Powerful transmitters can upset electronics; screening, bonding and filtering defend against it.
✈️ Pilot tip On refuelling, the bonding lead goes on first — before the cap comes off or the nozzle goes in.

🧑‍✈️ Electrical System Management 20 questions

Putting it together in the cockpit: reading the indications, deciding what to shed, and knowing what keeps working when the electrics do not.

🎯 Endurance sums🎯 What still works🎯 Smoke and fire🎯 One reset only🎯 Load priorities
💡 Key idea

Electrical failure is a time problem. Identify which source has gone, shed load to buy time, and land while the battery still has some to give.

  1. Recognise — warning light, ammeter discharge, low bus voltage, GEN OFF.
  2. Check and reset once — breaker, switch, one reset if permitted.
  3. Shed — keep aviate, navigate, communicate; drop comfort loads first.
  4. Plan — usable Ah ÷ load = time; land well inside it.

✅ Keeps working (typical piston single)

Engine on magnetos; airspeed, altimeter, VSI; vacuum-driven attitude and heading; magnetic compass.

❌ Lost

Radios and navigation, electric turn coordinator, fuel gauges, electric flaps and pumps, lights.

🔥 Smoke or burning smell Remove power (master off, if the checklist allows), then restore essentials one at a time while watching for the smoke to return. Do not reset a tripped fuel-pump breaker.
